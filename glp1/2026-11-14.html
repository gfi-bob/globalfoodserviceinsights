November 14, 2026

GLP-1 Weekly — the drugs, the users and the businesses they're reshaping

Covering GLP-1 weight-loss drug news from approximately November 4–14, 2026: drug development and regulation, public use and attitudes, products aimed at GLP-1 users, and what companies told investors about the drugs' impact.

1. The Drugs: Development, Approvals & Safety

  • China China's National Medical Products Administration approved Eli Lilly's once-daily pill orforglipron (Foundayo) for chronic weight management on Nov. 12, making it the first oral GLP-1 for obesity in China. It beats Novo Nordisk, whose oral Wegovy is still awaiting Chinese approval, into a market consultants L.E.K. expect to reach about $15 billion by 2030. Source: South China Morning Post, Reuters.
  • China Pfizer's ecnoglutide and Innovent's mazdutide were added to China's national reimbursement list for diabetes (reported Nov. 14), joining GLP-1s from Novo, Lilly and Guangzhou Innogen already on the list. Source: Reuters via MarketScreener.
  • United States The FDA ordered a new warning for NAION, a rare condition that cuts blood flow to the optic nerve and can cause permanent vision loss, on the labels of the whole GLP-1 class, including Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro and Zepbound (Nov. 9). The drugmakers say studies have not shown the drugs cause it. Source: Investing.com.
  • United States Lilly's Foundayo met its primary goal in the Phase 3 ATTAIN-OSA program for obstructive sleep apnea, cutting breathing interruptions during sleep by 43.8% in patients not using a CPAP-type machine and 27.5% in those who were, versus 6.3% and 3% on placebo. Lilly plans to file with the FDA for sleep apnea by year-end. The results were presented at the American Heart Association meeting on Nov. 9 and published in JAMA. Source: Eli Lilly, Fierce Pharma, Dow Jones.
  • United States Novo Nordisk said on Nov. 4 that it still expects an FDA decision on CagriSema, its semaglutide-plus-amylin combination injection, in the fourth quarter, with a U.S. launch planned for early 2027 if approved. Source: Novo Nordisk via Remy Research.
  • Global At the AASLD liver meeting, Merck posted a strong mid-stage result in MASH (fatty liver disease) for its own GLP-1/glucagon drug, a sign the GLP-1 field is widening beyond the two big incumbents. Source: Biotech Breakdowns weekly recap.

2. Usage & Public Attitudes

  • United States Circana's latest receipt-panel survey (released Nov. 10) found 30% of U.S. households report a current GLP-1 user and 37% say someone in the household has used one, up from 17% in 2024. Circana projects GLP-1 households could account for 40% of food and beverage units by 2030. Source: Circana. See our GLP-1 usage tracker.
  • United States Alvarez & Marsal's Fall 2026 consumer survey (Nov. 12) found 22% of respondents currently taking a GLP-1, up from 18% in the spring, with another 21% considering one. The firm says its figure runs above national estimates and should be read as a direction rather than a level. Sixty percent of users have bought "GLP-1 friendly" products, but 31% of users aged 45 and over say they have never seen them. Source: Alvarez & Marsal via GlobeNewswire.
  • United Kingdom Research from flavor house Givaudan (Nov. 10) puts GLP-1 users at 6.1% of U.K. adults and finds seven in ten users actively trying to improve their gut health, most by adding fiber. Source: NutraIngredients.
  • United States A Penn State national survey (Nov. 5) found current (25.8%) and former (26.8%) GLP-1 users were more likely than non-users (16.3%) to report frequent "food noise," the nagging thoughts about eating that the drugs are marketed to quiet. The researchers note this may be because people prone to food noise are the ones who seek the drugs. Source: StudyFinds.

3. Products Responding to GLP-1 Users

No new restaurant chain GLP-1 menu launch with a date in this window turned up after searching; McDonald's is still exploring "portion flexible" and "protein-led" options without an announced rollout. This week's product news came from telehealth and ingredient suppliers.

  • United States Lilly and telehealth platform Hims & Hers agreed on Nov. 6 to offer Lilly's drugs to self-pay patients, with Foundayo from $149 a month and Zepbound from $299 a month. Source: Kalkine, ChemXplore.
  • Europe Novonesis (Denmark) is studying a "unique cocktail" of pro-, pre- and post-biotics with Novo Nordisk as a companion product to help patients feel better during GLP-1 treatment, CEO Ester Baiget told CNBC on Nov. 9. Source: CNBC.
  • Europe Cosun Ingredients will show GLP-1 companion concepts, including a crunchy peanut bar and a ready-to-mix ube protein shake built on fiber and protein, at Fi Europe (Nov. 17–19). Source: Nutrition Insight.
  • United States At SupplySide Global, Arla Foods Ingredients showed three prototypes labeled "GLP-1 companion food": a high-protein shot, an unflavored protein water and snack bites. FrieslandCampina's stand read "Smaller appetites. Higher expectations." Source: All4Nutra.

4. On the Earnings Calls

  • Food — United States Flowers Foods (Q3, Nov. 13): net sales fell 4.7% to $1.169 billion on lower volume. CEO Ryals McMullian tied unit pressure at Dave's Killer Bread to GLP-1 use. He said the brand indexes highest among high-income consumers, who "are also the ones that use GLP-1s the most," adding, "I attribute a lot of that to the GLP-1 drugs." Source: Motley Fool transcript, Investing.com.
  • Food — United States Lifeway Foods (Q3, Nov. 12): record quarterly sales of $67.8 million, up about 19%. CEO Julie Smolyansky credited demand for kefir and high-protein farmer's cheese, citing tailwinds from gut health, protein and "GLP-1 dietary needs." Source: Yahoo Finance call highlights.
  • Pharma — Global Novo Nordisk (Q3, Nov. 4): obesity sales grew 22% and adjusted operating profit rose 27%, and the company raised 2026 guidance to −2% to +2% sales growth at constant currency. Its Wegovy pill holds about 80% of new-to-brand prescriptions in the oral obesity segment, but management still expects U.S. sales to decline under pricing pressure. Source: Novo Nordisk, Yahoo Finance.
  • Telehealth — United States Hims & Hers (Q3, Nov. 9): revenue rose 49% to $890.4 million, but the company swung to a $33 million net loss. Gross margin fell to 57% from 74% as its weight-loss business shifted from compounded to branded GLP-1s, and it cut its 2026 profit outlook for the third time this year. Source: Hims & Hers, Fierce Healthcare.
  • Pharma — United States Eli Lilly reported $184 million in Q3 sales of Foundayo, its oral GLP-1, while Novo's oral Wegovy still leads new prescriptions for obesity pills. Source: Fierce Pharma, Pharmaphorum.

A note on coverage: Items were checked against publication dates in the Nov. 4–14 window; the Novo–Lilly advertising lawsuit that resurfaced in coverage this week dates from July and is not included. Restaurant-menu news was thin this week (see section 3). Non-English sources searched included Chinese and Danish business press; the China approval and reimbursement items are drawn from English-language wire coverage of Chinese regulator announcements.
