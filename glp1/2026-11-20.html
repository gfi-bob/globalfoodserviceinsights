GLP-1 Weekly — the drugs, the users and the businesses they're reshaping

Covering GLP-1 drug, usage, consumer-product and earnings news worldwide from approximately November 10–20, 2026.

1. The Drugs: Development, Approvals & Safety

  • Global/Denmark Novo Nordisk presented new Phase 3 REDEFINE 9 results for CagriSema (cagrilintide plus semaglutide) at ObesityWeek (November 14–17) and in The Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology. In 309 adults with obesity, CagriSema produced mean weight loss of 20.7% (1.7 mg dose) and 21.0% (1 mg dose) at 68 weeks, versus 2.0% for pooled placebo — a roughly 19-point gap, with over 95% of drug-arm participants losing at least 5% of body weight versus 33.4% on placebo. Safety was consistent with the GLP-1 class (mostly mild-to-moderate GI events; no deaths); CagriSema is not yet FDA-approved for obesity, with a U.S. decision expected by year-end. Source: Healio.

3. Products Responding to GLP-1 Users

  • Global, trade/industry A food-innovation opinion piece argued that GLP-1-driven smaller portions are making nutrient density — protein, fiber, micronutrients — more important for "companion foods," and flagged taste/texture challenges (off-flavors and grittiness from some plant proteins at higher inclusion levels) that food formulators are working to solve through ingredient pairing and processing changes such as altered heating or homogenization methods. It also pointed to early-stage interest in gut-microbiome-support ingredients and high-protein pre-meal drinks intended to stimulate the body's own GLP-1 response. Published November 17. Source: FoodNavigator (opinion piece by René Floris, NIZO).

4. On the Earnings Calls

No new GLP-1-relevant earnings calls fell in this week's window. For context, two recent reports:

  • Eli Lilly, Q3 2026 (reported October 29): Zepbound worldwide sales reached $5.35 billion, up 49% year-over-year (U.S. sales $5.3 billion, up 48%), with volume up 53% even as realized prices fell 15%. CEO David Ricks said in the earnings release that "revenue growth was driven by broad global consumer demand for Mounjaro and Zepbound." Source: Medical Daily.
  • WeightWatchers, Q3 2026 (reported in early November; coverage dated November 6–9): Clinical subscription revenue — mainly its Med+/GLP-1 support offering — grew 48% year-over-year and now accounts for about 25% of total revenue, up from 15% a year ago, helped by a new Medicare Bridge program for GLP-1 access. CEO Stephen Bye said the company is "well-positioned to achieve sustainable, profitable growth in 2027." Source: Stockstory.
