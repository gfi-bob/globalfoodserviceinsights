Bob's Take · Week of November 20, 2026

Is This Thing Growing, Shrinking, or Just Moving to Shanghai?

By Bob O'Brien

Papa John's got a buyout offer this week — TriArtisan wants to take it private for something like $65 a share, call it $2.7 billion all in — and I hafta say that this seems pretty good given the bad press the foodservice industry has been getting recently. So, because I no longer have access to Circana’s real data about the global foodservice industry, I went and looked at Technomic's global chain data, unveiled at the industry's big confab in Cannes. They have somehow calculated that worldwide restaurant chain sales were up 5.2 percent nominal for 2026 — 1.9 percent once you strip out inflation. Asia-Pacific is doing the heavy lifting at 4 percent real growth, and China-based chains are up a startling 19.1 percent, with Luckin Coffee and Mixue doing most of the startling. Arcos Dorados, McDonald's biggest Latin American franchisee, posted revenue up 11.3 percent. The chains, broadly, are fine. It's the independents and the grocery aisle getting squeezed: store brands just hit a record 23.7 percent of units sold.

Meanwhile the marketing calendars are doing what marketing calendars do every November: escalating. Burger King's $19.54 advent calendar (priced to its founding year, because of course) has sold out in under five minutes the last two years running, so this year it's rationed to 110 a day. McDonald's brought Macaulay Culkin back for a Home Alone meal that comes with a themed sock — not a toy, a SOCK. Pizza Hut's $21.99 Triple Treat Box is in its eleventh year, Domino's wants $9.99 for a barbecue-bacon-pineapple pizza, and Starbucks just ran its annual Red Cup Day. Fine, seasonal, normal.

The more interesting fight is happening one aisle over: Novo Nordisk's CagriSema just posted a 68-week trial where patients lost roughly 20 percent of their body weight against 2 percent on placebo, and WeightWatchers says its GLP-1-linked "clinical" business grew 48 percent and is now a quarter of the whole company. Here in the global foodservice mountain redoubt, I'm quietly rewriting my own shopping list.
