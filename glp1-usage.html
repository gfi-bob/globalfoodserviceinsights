Bob's Take

Everybody's On It. Well, Not Everybody.

By Bob O'Brien

An American might think that everyone who can is taking a weight loss drug. Look around the office, the gym, the table next to you at dinner (go ahead, check what's left on their plate), and it can certainly feel that way. The surveys say otherwise, though not by as much as you might guess. About one American adult in nine is currently taking a GLP-1 to lose weight, and if you count the people taking them for diabetes or heart disease, it's closer to one in eight. That's a lot of people. It's also a long way from everybody.

And the U.S. is the outlier. Australia comes closest at roughly one adult in ten. Canada lands somewhere between one in twenty and one in twelve, depending on whose poll you believe. Britain has nearly doubled in under a year…to about 6 percent. Much of continental Europe sits around 2 percent, held back in places like Germany, where health insurers treat the drugs as a lifestyle choice. And in China and India, home to more than a third of the people on the planet, use rounds to…wait for it…zero. So the next time someone tells you GLP-1s are rewriting how the world eats, ask them which world they mean.

Source and method

Why there isn't a clean quarterly line. No government agency counts GLP-1 users, and the public polls that ask about current use run once or twice a year at most. Before 2024, no national poll asked whether adults were currently taking these drugs at all. Numerator surveys a fixed panel every quarter, but publishes household figures only occasionally; the full series is available to its clients.

Gallup (National Health and Well-Being Index, Gallup Panel): adults currently taking a GLP-1 for weight loss. The 2026 wave was fielded May 28–June 5, 2026 (n = 5,065). Gallup has not published field dates for its 2024 and 2025 waves; they are plotted in Q2 of each year and marked approximate. These are Gallup's revised figures after its September 2026 reweighting; an earlier Gallup release reported 5.8% for February 2024 and 12.4% for 2025 on a different basis.

KFF Health Tracking Poll: adults currently taking a GLP-1 for weight loss or a chronic condition such as diabetes or heart disease. Fielded April 23–May 1, 2024 (n = 1,479) and October 27–November 2, 2025 (n = 1,350).

NORC AmeriSpeak Omnibus: adults currently taking a GLP-1 for any reason, February 19–23, 2026 (n = 1,073).

Léger (LEO online panel, opt-in): U.S. adults currently taking a prescription GLP-1, February 6–9, 2026 (n = 1,012). An opt-in panel, so treat it as directional.

Households: Numerator's quarterly GLP-1 tracker reported 22% of U.S. households with a current user in its mid-2026 survey, double October 2023 (the 11% shown for Q4 2023 is derived from that "double"). Circana's receipt-panel surveys found 23% of households with a GLP-1 user in September 2025 and 30% with a current user in September 2026.
