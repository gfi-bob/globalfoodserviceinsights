Sources and definitions

Annual figures for the 13 OECD members come from the OECD's household consumption by purpose tables (current prices). Where the OECD breaks it out, the line used is food and beverage serving services, which is food away from home without hotels; for South Korea and New Zealand only restaurants and hotels is available. New Zealand's years end in March. Countries outside the OECD tables use national sources, listed below.

Quarterly figures come from each country's statistics office or industry body. Most measure restaurant and food-service sales in current prices. Where no spending or sales series exists, the closest proxy is shown hatched and labeled: restaurant price inflation (Mexico, South Korea), inflation-adjusted spending (Indonesia), or industry output (Nigeria). India publishes no comparable annual or quarterly series.

Because definitions differ by country, compare trends within a country more confidently than levels between countries.
