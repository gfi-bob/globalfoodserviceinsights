Consumer sentiment: how confident are households?

The OECD Consumer Confidence Indicator, built from each country's household survey and normalized so that every country's long-run average equals 100. Above 100 means consumers are more upbeat than usual for that country; below 100, more cautious. Because each country is measured against its own history, the trends can be compared across countries.

Source and method

Source: OECD, Composite Leading Indicators database, Consumer confidence indicator (CCI), amplitude adjusted, long-term average = 100, monthly. The OECD builds each country's indicator from its national consumer survey (for example, the University of Michigan survey for the United States and the European Commission survey for EU members), smooths it, and rescales it so the long-run average is 100 and swings are comparable in size across countries.

Annual and quarterly values are simple averages of the monthly readings. The latest month published is shown for each country in the table.

Not covered: Nigeria, Singapore and Taiwan are not in the OECD indicator. Their national surveys (Central Bank of Nigeria Consumer Expectations Survey; National Central University's Taiwan consumer confidence index) use different scales, so they are left out rather than mixed in un-normalized.
